# Klárka Pavlînka and Raphael — Emotional Conversation About Simonka Káta and Holic

## Scene Outline

## Placement

**When:** Monday evening, after the first day of the new school year. The family has already learned that Simonka Káta chose a relationship with Holic Bartunek.

**Where:** Klárka and Raphael's apartment in Prague, after the academy has closed.

**Pregnancy Continuity:** Klárka is in the **early stage of pregnancy** with her and Raphael's first child. The pregnancy is confirmed, but the precise week, whether the wider family knows, and whether they have seen an ultrasound can remain open. It should be physically present through fatigue, nausea, protectiveness, and Klárka's hand unconsciously resting on her stomach—not through a visibly advanced pregnancy or fetal movement.

**Ages:** Klárka is about twenty-four; Simonka is about twenty-three. Both sisters are adults.

---

## Immediate Lead-In

- The family has learned enough to understand that Simonka is involved with Holic Bartunek and has described the choice as her own.
- Klárka has read or been told enough to understand that Simonka has entered a highly private, structured relationship that Klárka does not understand.
- The first day back at the academy has forced Klárka to perform ordinary competence while carrying fear about a private life she cannot enter or interpret.
- Klárka comes home having held herself together through academy responsibilities. Raphael sees immediately that she is past ordinary tiredness.

---

## Scene Purpose

This scene gives Klárka and Raphael a private emotional reckoning that is distinct from the family crisis scenes.

Klárka is not asking Raphael to solve Simonka's life. She is trying to reconcile three truths that her body and heart cannot yet hold together:

1. **Simonka is an adult who says she made this choice.**
2. **Klárka is deeply frightened by the imbalance, isolation, and secrecy surrounding Holic's world.**
3. **Klárka's own pregnancy has made the limits of protection suddenly unbearable.**

Raphael's function is not to defend Holic or correct Klárka's feelings. He helps her distinguish between fear and condemnation, between keeping a route home open and trying to control Simonka, and between being a loving sister and being responsible for another adult's choices.

By the end of the scene, Klárka has not accepted or understood Simonka's relationship. She has arrived at a more useful, painful position:

> *I can be scared. I can disagree. I can still leave the light on.*

---

## Core Emotional Conflict

Klárka has spent her life fifteen months ahead of Simonka. She is accustomed to being the elder sister who goes first, notices things, makes space, and comes when Simonka calls.

But Simonka's involvement with Holic reveals a part of Simonka's adult life Klárka cannot enter, supervise, or make safe by force.

Pregnancy intensifies that discovery. Klárka has begun to think about protecting a child before that child is even born; at the same time, she is confronting the fact that she cannot protect the sister she already has by making choices for her.

The scene's central question is therefore not simply, *Is Holic dangerous?*

It is:

> *How do you love someone fiercely without turning your love into another form of control?*

---

## Characters

### Klárka Pavlînka

- Early in pregnancy with her and Raphael's first child.
- Physically depleted after a day at the academy; she may be nauseated, overtired, or emotionally raw in ways she cannot fully separate from the pregnancy.
- Simonka's older sister by fifteen months and lifelong best friend.
- Knows Simonka is an adult and has said she chose Holic, but emotionally cannot make that fact feel safe.
- Afraid of Holic's apparent authority, resources, confidence, and ability to create a self-contained world around Simonka.
- Carries an unspoken fear that she failed to notice her sister's need before Holic did.
- Her anger is protective and frightened, not a final or objective judgment of Simonka.

### Raphael

- Klárka's partner and the father of her unborn child.
- Knows this is a conversation about Simonka's private life, not an invitation to consume private details.
- Does not pretend to know whether Holic is good or evil based on fragmentary information.
- Takes concerns about power, isolation, and Simonka's ability to leave seriously.
- Does not lecture Klárka about adult autonomy while she is in distress.
- Practices the family habit of asking before touching; his physical support is offered, never assumed.
- Helps Klárka find an actionable posture: honest concern, a non-shaming message, and an open route back.

### Simonka Káta — Absent Presence

- Present through the diary/disclosure and through the silence of an unread or unanswered phone.
- Her apparent normality is crucial: she does not look like someone visibly in crisis, so Klárka cannot use appearance as a simple answer to the question of safety.
- Her adulthood and stated consent are real, while the family remains entitled to ask whether she has privacy, support, and a genuine ability to leave.

### Holic Bartunek — Absent Force

- Never appears in the scene.
- Exists only through what Klárka knows and fears: a man with more experience, authority, and control than Simonka's family can easily understand.
- The outline must not declare him abusive or harmless. The family has incomplete information.
- Raphael can state the important distinction: consent is meaningful, but a loving family can still care about power, isolation, and whether consent can be withdrawn without punishment.

---

## Setting and Atmosphere

- It is raining or has recently rained; Prague is damp, quiet, and ordinary outside the windows.
- The apartment is low-lit—one lamp rather than the overhead lights.
- Klárka comes in still wearing some part of her academy day: training clothes, a cardigan, or her hair not yet fully taken down.
- Her bag is left unpacked. This is a visual sign that she has not transitioned out of crisis mode.
- Raphael has prepared nothing dramatic. The domestic normalcy—water, tea, a blanket, a lamp, a coat over a chair—contrasts with the size of the feeling.
- Klárka eventually sits on the floor beside the sofa or curls into one corner of it. Raphael lets her choose the distance between them.

**Key image:** Klárka's hand resting unconsciously over her early pregnancy while she talks about the sister she cannot protect.

---

## Scene Structure

### Beat 1 — Raphael Finds Klárka Holding Herself Together Too Hard

- Raphael comes home or finds Klárka already home, sitting in the dim apartment without having changed, eaten, or unpacked her bag.
- He recognizes the difference between ordinary academy fatigue and the contained stillness that means she is close to breaking.
- He does not begin with questions about Simonka or Holic.
- He offers concrete choices: tea, water, quiet, company.
- Klárka rejects the first offers, then accepts only his presence.

**Purpose:** Establishes Raphael as attentive but non-intrusive. Klárka must choose the conversation rather than be pressed into it.

**Possible dialogue:**

> **Raphael:** Do you want me to sit here?
>
> **Klárka:** Just sit. Don't make me explain yet.

---

### Beat 2 — Fifteen Months Apart

- Klárka tries to explain why the situation feels so personal and destabilizing.
- She recalls being fifteen months older: first steps, first classes, tram rides, family holidays, shared childhood rituals, and the sense that Simonka was always just behind her.
- She recognizes that the old-sister role taught her to believe she would notice when Simonka was in trouble.
- The hidden relationship makes her feel she failed at a job nobody actually gave her.

**Key line:**

> I was only fifteen months ahead of her. How did she get so far away without me seeing it?

**Raphael's response:** He does not dismiss the bond. He gently says that Simonka's secrecy is not proof Klárka did not love or observe her well enough; it is proof that Simonka had something she could not yet say aloud.

---

### Beat 3 — Klárka Names Her Fear of Holic

- Klárka's grief shifts into anger because anger is easier to stand inside.
- She names what frightens her about Holic without treating speculation as fact:
 - He is older or more experienced in this world.
 - He has more money, confidence, privacy, and practical power.
 - His home and rules create a world Simonka may be tempted to disappear into.
 - Simonka's relationship with him is opaque to the family.
- Klárka fears that Holic can offer Simonka structure, certainty, and a place to stop explaining herself—things that may feel like safety even if they also create dependency.
- She says that she wants to make Holic into a simple villain because then rescue would be simple.

**Important characterization:** Klárka does not need to be right in every fear. The scene lets her speak fear honestly without turning it into a factual verdict.

**Possible dialogue:**

> **Klárka:** I want to call him a monster because then I would know what to do. I would go get her. I would bring her home. I would make it simple.
>
> **Raphael:** And you know it may not be simple.
>
> **Klárka:** That is what I hate.

---

### Beat 4 — Raphael Holds Both Consent and Concern

- Raphael asks permission before moving closer or touching Klárka.
- Once she agrees, he comforts her physically without trying to quiet her too soon.
- He validates the questions behind her fear:
 - Is Simonka safe?
 - Is she isolated from people who know her outside the relationship?
 - Can she say no or leave without retaliation, humiliation, or loss of support?
 - Does she have an independent way to call family, get home, and speak privately?
- He does **not** claim to know the answers.
- He also reminds Klárka that Simonka's consent and intelligence cannot be erased because the family finds her decision painful or unfamiliar.

**Core Raphael line:**

> She can be an adult making a choice, and you can still ask whether she is safe inside it. Those are not opposite things.

**Purpose:** Positions Raphael as ethically grounded. He neither treats Simonka as incapable nor tells Klárka to suppress her concern.

---

### Beat 5 — Pregnancy Makes the Limit of Protection Real

- Klárka's hand moves to her stomach; she may be surprised by the gesture herself.
- She says plainly that she is pregnant, not as new information for Raphael but as a fact that has changed the scale of every fear.
- She confesses that learning how much of Simonka's life can remain unknowable has terrified her about becoming a mother.
- She asks how anyone lets a child become separate: leave home, make bad choices, withhold things, or walk toward people their family does not understand.
- The admission may be tearful and raw:

> How do I protect our baby if I cannot even protect Simonka?

- Raphael answers without false reassurance. He says Klárka cannot guarantee a child will never hurt, hide, leave, or choose something she would not choose.
- He reframes the family task: not total prevention, but building a home in which truth can return.

**Possible dialogue:**

> **Raphael:** You will not be able to make our child transparent to us.
>
> **Klárka:** That is a terrible thing to say to a pregnant woman.
>
> **Raphael:** It is. But it is also why we make a home where they never have to earn their way back into it.

---

### Beat 6 — The Fear of Becoming a Cage

- Raphael's language makes Klárka confront the part of her that wants to take Simonka home and refuse to let her leave again.
- Klárka recoils from the idea of becoming controlling, especially given her mother's history and the family's values around bodily autonomy.
- She says:

> I do not want to be another cage.

- Raphael makes clear that fear itself does not make her controlling. What matters is what she does with it.
- He distinguishes between:
 - demanding that Simonka end the relationship;
 - shaming or ridiculing her;
 - treating her private life as family property;
 - versus telling her honestly that she is loved, that the family is worried, and that help is available without punishment.

**Purpose:** This is the moral center of the scene. Klárka chooses not to turn her love into coercion even though every protective instinct wants certainty.

---

### Beat 7 — A Message That Does Not Close the Door

- Raphael suggests one practical next step: a text or voice message to Simonka.
- Klárka resists because she fears sounding fake, controlling, or helpless.
- They build the message together. It should not demand explanations, insist Simonka come home, or pretend that Klárka approves of what she does not understand.
- It must tell the truth without imposing a condition on contact.

**Recommended message:**

> *Simonko, I am scared because I love you. I will not pretend I understand everything, and I will not stop being your sister. If you need me, I will come. If you do not want me to come, I will still answer the phone. You never have to earn your way back to us.*

- Klárka worries that Simonka will hate the message or not respond.
- Raphael says that sending an honest, non-shaming message is still worthwhile even without an immediate reply.

**Key emotional result:** Klárka gets one action that expresses care without claiming authority over Simonka's decision.

---

### Beat 8 — Leaving a Light On

- The active conversation exhausts Klárka. She sits with Raphael, crying more quietly or simply breathing against him.
- Raphael reaches to turn off the lamp when they prepare for bed.
- Klárka stops him and asks him to leave it on, at least for the night.
- Neither person needs to explain the symbolism. It belongs to the broader family language: door cracked, porch light on, someone ready to answer.
- Klárka holds one hand over her stomach and keeps her phone in the other or nearby.

**Closing image:**

> A small lamp remains on in the Prague apartment. Klárka cannot reach Simonka, cannot decide for her, and cannot guarantee what Holic's world means. But she does not close her hand around either fear. The phone is charged. The light is on. The door remains open.

---

## Key Dialogue Possibilities

### Klárka

> I have known her every day of my life. How can there be a whole world in her that I cannot find?

> I want to call him a monster because then I would know what to do.

> She says she chose it. Why does that not make me less afraid?

> I am angry with her for making me carry this, and I hate myself for being angry.

> I am pregnant, Raphael. How am I supposed to become a mother when I am learning I cannot protect everyone I love?

> I do not want to be another cage.

> I can be scared because I love her. I can say that much.

### Raphael

> Do you want me to sit here?

> You do not have to explain before you are ready.

> She can be an adult making a choice, and you can still ask whether she is safe inside it.

> We do not know enough to call him harmless. We do not know enough to call him a monster. We do know enough to make sure she has a way to reach us.

> Your fear does not make you controlling. What you do with your fear matters.

> We cannot love a person into being transparent to us.

> We can make a home where they do not have to earn their way back.

> Send the message you can stand behind tomorrow, even if she does not answer tomorrow.

---

## What This Scene Establishes

1. **Klárka's pregnancy is emotionally integrated into the Simonka/Holic arc.** It raises—not resolves—her fear about the limits of love and protection.
2. **Raphael is a trusted partner.** He comforts Klárka, respects Simonka's private boundaries, and offers ethical steadiness rather than simplistic answers.
3. **The family can be concerned without denying Simonka's adulthood.** Consent, care, privacy, unequal power, and the ability to leave are treated as related but separate questions.
4. **Klárka does not become Simonka's rescuer or jailer.** She chooses a difficult posture: honesty, availability, and continued connection.
5. **The door/light motif expands into Klárka's own future family.** As an expectant mother, she begins to understand the family inheritance not as control, but as a promise of return.
6. **This scene can lead into a Simonka–Klárka text exchange, a later face-to-face sister conversation, or a wider family scene.**

---

## Continuity Notes

- Keep the scene **non-explicit**. It is about family love, fear, autonomy, pregnancy, and the emotional consequences of secrecy—not the mechanics of Simonka and Holic's relationship.
- Raphael does not read, quote, or ask for private diary details.
- Do not make a definitive diagnosis of Holic's character from partial information. Klárka's fears are valid emotional data, not necessarily conclusive evidence.
- Do not let Raphael use consent as a way to dismiss Klárka. His role is to hold both truths: Simonka has agency, and people who love her may still care about safety and isolation.
- The exact point in Klárka's pregnancy is intentionally open. Use early-pregnancy symptoms and gestures rather than a visible late-pregnancy body or fetal movement.
- This scene fits after the family's initial discovery of Simonka's relationship and before any final resolution involving Simonka or Holic.
