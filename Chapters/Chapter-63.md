# Chapter Sixty-Three — Wednesday at the Whistler Mansion

## 1. Wednesday afternoon

- The chapter takes place on Wednesday afternoon, one day before the planned flight to Kyoto.
- Mike takes Terri Anne and eight-month-old Patti Anne to the family's mansion in Whistler, British Columbia.
- The visit gives them a private place to pause before the international trip.
- Mike also wants to review the legal requirements for Terri Anne's two proposed films in Japan.

## 2. Arrival in Whistler

- Terri Anne looks at the mountain setting as they approach the mansion.
- The Whistler property feels different from the Carlisle mansion but is another home available to Mike's family.
- Mike reminds Terri Anne that she and Patti Anne are not merely temporary guests in his homes.
- Staff make certain that Patti Anne has a safe place to crawl and rest.

## 3. Patti Anne explores another home

- Terri Anne places Patti Anne on a clean, safe section of the mansion floor.
- Patti Anne crawls while Mike and Terri Anne remain close enough to watch her.
- Mike again treats the mansion as a home rather than a museum.
- The ordinary family moment contrasts with the complicated legal conversation ahead.

## 4. Mike begins the conversation

- Mike tells Terri Anne that filming an adult movie in Japan is governed by Japanese law, not merely Key Porn Studios policy.
- Being eighteen makes Terri Anne an adult, but it does not allow a studio to ignore performer-protection requirements.
- Mike says the company must satisfy Japanese contract, filming, release, immigration, and obscenity rules.
- He does not want Terri Anne boarding the airplane without understanding her legal rights.

## 5. Each movie needs its own agreement

- Mike explains that Japan's 2022 adult-video performer-protection law requires a separate performance agreement for each sexually explicit video.
- The agreement must be written or electronically recorded to become effective.
- Terri Anne's movie with two adult women and the proposed movie with ten adult men therefore require separate contracts.
- A broad agreement allowing the studio to place her into any production would not be sufficient.

## 6. The contract must be specific

- Mike explains that each contract must identify the producer and performer.
- It must state the scheduled time, date, and filming location.
- It must explain the specific content to be filmed and identify the other performers involved.
- It must also state how and when the movie will be released, Terri Anne's compensation, and when she will be paid.

## 7. The studio must explain the contract

- The producer must give Terri Anne a draft and explain it thoroughly and clearly.
- She must receive both the contract and the legally required explanatory information.
- Mike says Terri Anne is entitled to an explanation she can genuinely understand, not hurried paperwork placed in front of her after arrival.
- He offers access to independent legal advice and a qualified interpreter rather than relying only on the studio's representatives.

## 8. The one-month waiting period

- Mike explains the rule that immediately affects their travel plan.
- Filming cannot begin until one month has passed from the later date on which Terri Anne receives the contract or the required explanatory document.
- The waiting period applies even when a performer says she is certain and wants to begin sooner.
- A last-minute replacement cannot simply sign on Wednesday and lawfully film that Saturday in Japan.

## 9. Terri Anne thought she could film on Saturday

- Terri Anne tells Mike that she thought she could be filming on Saturday instead of waiting one month.
- She accepted the Kyoto movie believing that the proposed weekend schedule was legally possible.
- Mike understands why she believed that because the production was presented to her as an immediate replacement opportunity.
- He explains that a studio's schedule or offer cannot override Japan's mandatory waiting period.
- Terri Anne only accepted the Kyoto movie with the ten adult men during the week of the proposed shoot.
- Mike tells her that the new production cannot lawfully film her on Saturday if the required contract and explanation were not provided at least one month earlier.
- Moving her original movie with the two women to Kyoto does not eliminate the rule; each production must independently comply.
- Mike says the legal team must verify when Terri Anne received the paperwork for the original movie as well.
- Mike asks Terri Anne whether she wants to forget both movies entirely.
- He assures her that she can withdraw from the movie with the two women and the movie with the ten men without any penalties.
- Mike does not tell her what to choose; he makes clear that the decision belongs to Terri Anne.
- Terri Anne considers the offer before answering.

## 10. Terri Anne worries about Christmas

- Terri Anne tells Mike that she is worried about Christmas.
- She does not have enough money to buy Christmas presents for Patti Anne.
- Her concern helps Mike understand why the income from the proposed movies feels important to her.
- Terri Anne wants to give her daughter a happy first Christmas without feeling financially dependent or inadequate as Patti Anne's mother.

## 11. Mike reassures Terri Anne

- Mike holds Patti Anne securely while moving close enough to hug Terri Anne.
- He tells her, **“We are a couple, so we can buy Christmas presents for Patti Anne together without any doubt.”**
- Mike presents the gifts as something they will choose as Patti Anne's parents, not as a debt Terri Anne will owe him.
- Terri Anne asks Mike whether he is certain.
- Mike tells her that he is certain because he loves Patti Anne and Terri Anne so much.
- Terri Anne begins crying as the reassurance sinks in.
- She tells Mike that he is unbelievable.
- Her tears come from relief and from realizing that she no longer has to face Patti Anne's first Christmas alone.
- Terri Anne tells Mike that she loves him so much.

## 12. The Christmas tree will wait for them

- Mike tells Terri Anne that he already gave Wolfee instructions not to put up the Christmas tree before they return home.
- He wants Terri Anne and Patti Anne to be present when the Carlisle mansion is decorated.
- The tree will not be treated as another mansion decision made without Terri Anne.
- Mike wants them to begin Patti Anne's first Christmas together as a family.

## 13. Terri Anne asks about travelling around Japan

- Terri Anne asks Mike whether she and Patti Anne can travel around Japan until the one-month waiting period is over.
- She wants to know whether they must remain in Kyoto for the entire month.
- Her question turns the legal delay into a possible opportunity for Patti Anne and her to experience another country together.

## 14. Mike offers them more of Japan

- Mike tells Terri Anne that she and Patti Anne do not have to remain in Kyoto.
- They can travel around Japan and see its different and unique cities while waiting for any lawful filming date.
- Mike explains that the family owns another mansion in Sapporo.
- The Sapporo mansion has its own private onsen.
- Mike explains that he rebuilt the entire onsen indoors and redesigned its surrounding deck because of Ashlee.
- The accessible indoor layout allows Ashlee to approach and use the onsen safely with her wheelchair and support equipment.
- Patti Anne may also enter the onsen when an adult holds her continuously and the water has first been adjusted to an infant-safe temperature.
- They also have a penthouse in Tokyo.
- The Sapporo mansion and Tokyo penthouse give them secure places to stay while exploring Japan.
- Mike treats the month as family travel rather than pressuring Terri Anne to wait beside the Kyoto studio.

## 15. Patti Anne falls asleep on Mike's shoulder

- While Mike is holding her, Patti Anne settles against his right shoulder.
- For the first time, she falls asleep on Mike's shoulder.
- Mike remains still and supports her securely so he does not wake her.
- Terri Anne watches her daughter sleeping comfortably against him and recognizes another new bond forming between Mike and Patti Anne.

## 16. Terri Anne chooses both movies

- Terri Anne tells Mike that she would like to make both movies in thirty days.
- She wants to complete the movie with the two adult women and the second movie with the ten adult men.
- Her decision is made after Mike explains that she can walk away from both productions without penalties.
- Mike reminds her that the Japanese legal team must calculate the first lawful filming date from the later date on which she receives the required contract and explanatory documents.
- Terri Anne's reference to thirty days means after the complete statutory one-month waiting period has expired, not before.
- The contracts, work authorization, safety arrangements, and filming schedule must all be approved before either production proceeds.

## 17. Mike will not ignore the waiting period

- Mike tells Terri Anne that owning Key Porn Studios does not place him or the company above Japanese law.
- He will not ask her to waive a protection that the statute does not allow the producer to bypass.
- If the timing is noncompliant, the films must be postponed, relocated, or otherwise restructured lawfully.
- The planned Thursday flight may still become a family or business trip, but it cannot be used to conceal unauthorized filming.

## 18. Terri Anne may refuse any part

- Mike explains that even if a particular act appears in a valid contract, Terri Anne may refuse all or part of the filming.
- She is not liable for the producer's or another person's financial loss because she refuses.
- A contract is not permanent consent, and agreeing before the shoot does not remove her right to stop during it.
- Mike tells her that this protection is especially important in a production involving many other performers.

## 19. Health, safety, and sanitation

- Japanese law requires special consideration for the performer's physical health, reproductive health, safety, and sanitation.
- The production must take measures that preserve the voluntary nature of her participation.
- Terri Anne must be able to pause or refuse without being cornered by the schedule, travel costs, or the number of people waiting.
- Mike says the proposed four-hour break cannot replace a proper medical and safety plan.

## 20. Terri Anne may review the footage

- Before release, Terri Anne must receive an opportunity to review the footage of her performance that the producer intends to publish.
- Mike explains that the review right helps her understand what the final production will show.
- The studio cannot lawfully treat the recorded images as something she is never allowed to see.
- Key Porn Studios must document that the opportunity was genuinely provided.

## 21. The legal minimum and the studio's six-month release policy

- Mike tells Terri Anne that Japanese law requires at least four months between completion of filming and release.
- Key Porn Studios applies its longer company-wide policy and does not release any movie on its website until six months after filming.
- The six-month studio policy therefore controls Terri Anne's actual release date.
- This delay is separate from the one-month period required before filming.
- The studio must account for both waiting periods in its production schedule.
- A rushed shoot cannot be followed by an immediate Japanese release.

## 22. Cancellation rights

- Mike explains that Japanese law gives a performer broad rights to withdraw from or cancel a performance agreement in writing or electronically.
- In general, that at-will right continues until one year after the movie is released.
- The producer cannot impose damages merely because the performer exercises the statutory right.
- If the producer violates key duties, the performer has additional cancellation and takedown remedies.

## 23. Japanese and international versions

- Mike explains that Article 175 of Japan's Penal Code restricts distributing, selling, or publicly displaying obscene material.
- The statute does not literally prescribe a particular pixel pattern.
- In practice, Japanese adult-video producers use mosaic censorship over visible genitalia to comply with court interpretations and industry review standards.
- Terri Anne asks Mike whether her first movie—the film with the two adult women—is intended for a Japanese audience.
- Mike tells her that the first movie is not for the Japanese audience; it is intended for distribution in other countries.
- Filming that movie in Japan still requires compliance with Japanese performer-protection and immigration law even though its intended audience is outside Japan.
- The second Kyoto movie is intended to be released in other countries as well as Japan.
- In countries where local law permits it, an international version may be released without the Japanese mosaic and may show everything recorded for the finished movie.
- Terri Anne's contract must clearly explain the international distribution plan so she understands that viewers outside Japan may see an uncensored version.
- Any version distributed in Japan must pass the appropriate Japanese legal and compliance review.
- International distribution does not eliminate the one-month waiting period or Terri Anne's other protections because the filming takes place in Japan.

## 24. Immigration and work authorization

- Mike explains that a passport only allows Terri Anne to travel; it does not automatically authorize paid filming in Japan.
- Paid commercial filming by a foreign performer ordinarily requires the correct Japanese work status, commonly the Entertainer status of residence, and properly documented activities.
- Travelling as a tourist and secretly performing paid work could create immigration consequences.
- Mike will require Japanese immigration counsel to confirm that Terri Anne, the other performers, and any foreign crew have proper authorization before anyone works.

## 25. Patti Anne's travel arrangements

- Patti Anne needs her own valid travel documents to enter Japan.
- Mike and Terri Anne must also carry the documents needed to show that Terri Anne is Patti Anne's mother and is authorized to travel with her.
- Childcare arrangements in Kyoto must remain completely separate from the adult production areas.
- Mike promises that Patti Anne's safety will not be improvised around a filming schedule.

## 26. Mike distinguishes support from pressure

- Mike tells Terri Anne that he supports her right to choose adult-film work.
- Supporting her does not mean pretending that every proposed production is safe or lawful.
- He will not decide for her whether she wants the work, but he will insist that Key Porn Studios obey the law.
- Terri Anne understands that Mike is giving her information rather than ordering her to abandon her career.

## 27. The Kyoto plan must be reviewed

- Mike says they need immediate answers from Charlene and the Japanese legal team.
- They must verify the contract dates, separate agreements, performer identities, filming details, release plans, safety measures, visas, and Kyoto facility compliance.
- The original Saturday filming plan cannot proceed if the one-month waiting period has not expired.
- Terri Anne's decision allows the legal team to begin scheduling both productions for the first lawful date approximately one month later.
- Mike will not allow wealth, private aircraft, or corporate influence to be used as substitutes for legal compliance.

## 28. Closing beat

- Patti Anne remains asleep against Mike's right shoulder.
- Terri Anne looks at both of them and repeats that she wants to make the two movies after the full one-month waiting period.
- Thursday's planned flight may continue as family travel while the legal and immigration teams prepare a lawful production date.
- The chapter ends at the Whistler mansion with Mike preparing to call Charlene and Key Porn Studios' Japanese counsel.
