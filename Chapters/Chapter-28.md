# Chapter Twenty-Eight — Captain Morrison

## 1. Captain Morrison arrives

- During one of Scout's shifts at the Blue Velvet Room, a familiar customer enters the club.
- His name is **Captain Morrison**.
- The employees recognize him as one of Scout's regular customers.
- He uses a wheelchair because he lost both legs during a war.
- Captain Morrison moves through the club independently and expects to be treated with the same respect as any other customer.

## 2. Scout welcomes him

- Scout notices Captain Morrison and greets him warmly.
- Her kindness is genuine rather than a performance for a customer.
- Captain Morrison is pleased to see her and asks how she has been doing.
- Scout makes certain that they have a comfortable place to talk without treating his wheelchair as a burden.

## 3. Scout's connection to military service

- Scout has always felt a personal respect for Captain Morrison because both of her grandfathers served in the army.
- Their service shaped the way she thinks about veterans and the sacrifices made by military families.
- She does not pretend that her grandfathers' experiences allow her to understand everything Captain Morrison survived.
- Instead, that family connection encourages her to listen to him with care.

## 4. Captain Morrison's government allowance

- Scout knows that Captain Morrison lives on a limited government allowance.
- The amount leaves him with little money after paying his regular expenses.
- He occasionally visits the Blue Velvet Room for companionship and a temporary escape from daily pressures.
- Scout never mocks his financial limitations or makes him feel unwelcome because he cannot spend like wealthier customers.

## 5. Scout's discount

- Scout gives Captain Morrison a discount on private dances.
- She does so because she knows how little he receives from his government allowance.
- The discount allows him to enjoy his visit without sacrificing too much of the money he needs for everyday life.
- Scout handles the arrangement discreetly so that other customers do not embarrass him or treat him as a charity case.

## 6. Respect rather than pity

- Captain Morrison values Scout's kindness but does not want pity.
- Scout treats the discount as an arrangement between two people who respect one another.
- She speaks directly to him rather than addressing someone else because he uses a wheelchair.
- Their interaction shows that Scout sees him as a whole person, not only as a veteran or a man who lost his legs.

## 7. A familiar private dance

- Captain Morrison asks whether Scout has time for one of their usual private dances.
- Scout agrees and confirms the discounted price.
- She ensures that the private area is accessible and that he can position his wheelchair comfortably.
- Their established boundaries allow both of them to feel at ease.

## 8. Captain Morrison notices a change

- Captain Morrison senses that Scout seems different from the last time he visited.
- Adam and Don are gone, and the club's atmosphere feels less threatening.
- Scout appears happier but also distracted by the changes in her relationship with Mike.
- Captain Morrison does not pry, but his observation reminds Scout that regular customers notice more than employees sometimes realize.

## 9. Charlene and Marco observe

- Charlene and Marco see how Scout treats Captain Morrison.
- They recognize that her kindness has helped build customer loyalty while preserving his dignity.
- Neither interferes with the respectful arrangement between them.
- The interaction offers Charlene another example of Scout's empathy and professionalism.

## 10. Closing beat

- Scout and Captain Morrison continue their familiar visit under the Blue Velvet Room's new management.
- For Captain Morrison, the club remains a place where he can enjoy companionship without being reduced to his disability or limited income.
- For Scout, his visit reconnects her with the values she learned from her grandfathers' military service.
- The chapter ends with mutual respect rather than pity defining their relationship.

## Chapter Purpose

This chapter:

- Introduces Captain Morrison as one of Scout's regular customers.
- Establishes that he uses a wheelchair after losing both legs in a war.
- Connects Scout's kindness to her grandfathers' army service.
- Shows Scout giving him discounted private dances because of his limited government allowance.
- Portrays Captain Morrison with independence and dignity.
- Demonstrates Scout's empathy and professionalism under the club's new management.
