## Confirmed Facts

- When Madeleine Jaycee Key is three, Carlota Delicia and Keith ask Taylor Raven and Adelisa to become her godmothers, partly because Adelisa’s juvenile idiopathic arthritis means additional support may be important. Carlota Delicia and Keith legally designate both women as Madeleine Jaycee Key’s guardians if they die. Carlota Delicia and Keith are killed in a vehicle crash when Madeleine Jaycee Key is fifteen and she and Caesarius Lavigne are preparing for their first Winter Olympic Games. Madeleine Jaycee Key continues living happily in Orlando with Caesarius Lavigne and Adelisa, who provides day-to-day care. Taylor Raven shares legal guardianship, provides continuing emotional support, and remains available for Madeleine Jaycee Key to call at any time. Chairat Narissara is happy that Taylor Raven is helping her niece, Madeleine Jaycee Key.
- Carlota Delicia and Keith marry before their deaths.
- Carlota Delicia and Keith are killed in a vehicle crash.
- The crash and deaths remain non-graphic.
- Carlota Delicia and Keith are killed when Madeleine Jaycee Key is fifteen and she and Caesarius Lavigne are preparing for their first Winter Olympic Games representing Canada in pair skating.
- That Winter Olympic Games is hosted in a city in the United States of America.
- An announcer in the host city delivers the breaking news to the world.
- The announcer identifies Carlota Delicia as one of Mike Key’s granddaughters, Keith as her husband, and Madeleine Jaycee Key as one of Mike Key’s great-granddaughters.
- The announcer says: **“Breaking news: One of Mike Key’s granddaughters, Carlota Delicia, and her husband, Keith, have been killed in a vehicle crash. One of Mike Key’s great-granddaughters, Madeleine Jaycee Key, will compete in pair skating at these Winter Olympic Games with her deaf partner, Caesarius Lavigne. There is no chance for this pair to win anything at these Games. What the hell is the Canadian team thinking by allowing them to be on this team?”**
- The announcer’s remarks about Caesarius Lavigne’s deafness and the pair’s ability are explicitly discriminatory and are not treated as an accurate assessment of the athletes.
